DICOMs, using the existing slice selection, intensity normalization and acquired
orientation policy. Do not overwrite the 224 cache or enlarge it to create
the candidate. Audit original Rows/Columns across the full inventory first:
the local 30 DICOMs are all 800x800 but belong to only one series.

At 24,371 series, uncompressed image payload is approximately 80.32 GiB,
versus 27.33 GiB at 224. Six evenly sized shards average 13.39 GiB each;
actual assignment must enforce a byte budget with room for metadata and outputs.
384 has 2.94 times as many pixels per slice; runtime and GPU-memory multipliers
must be measured rather than assumed equal to this ratio.

Use ConvNeXt-V1 with protocol selection, 16 slices and five series, unchanged
labels and 12-epoch schedule. Compare fresh 224 and 384 controls on fold 1
with matched initial weights, split and effective batch size. If microbatch
size changes, align optimizer updates and accumulation across both arms.
Keep slice transformers and auxiliary losses disabled to isolate resolution.

Before full training, verify raw-to-cache/inference equality, source-cache
geometry in checkpoint metadata, and separation of the side classifier's
224-pixel inputs. The current artifact loader rejects different cache geometry;
mixed-resolution inference requires explicit support and tests, not a global
replacement of CACHE_SIZE in the submission.
