# Application clarifications for the frozen research rubric

Apply these checks while reading the entire report. They clarify extraction;
they do not establish new competition thresholds or change the rubric.

- Reconcile findings and impression before assigning a state. If equally
  specific descriptions differ in certainty, preserve uncertainty. A suspected
  or probable tear is not definite present. A likely cause or age of a clearly
  asserted finding does not by itself make the finding uncertain.
- Keep negation within its stated anatomy. A general unremarkable impression
  does not repair a duplicated ligament name or negate every omitted finding.
  Normal marrow signal alone does not explicitly negate fracture. A statement
  that bones/osseous structures are normal can cover those named structures.
- Use specific abnormal statements over blanket normal descriptions. Grade-II
  meniscal degeneration without a tear remains below the meniscal-tear threshold.
- Do not turn plica thickening, isolated bursitis, fat-pad edema or a cyst in a
  different structure into an unstated target finding.
- Explicit tricompartmental OA applies to the three named OA labels. Keep
  compartment attribution and severity separate. Preserve unresolved low-grade
  ACL, cartilage and trace-fluid thresholds as uncertainty where the rubric
  cannot decide them; do not invent positive or negative certainty.
- Present and negated states use definite certainty. All evidence quotations
  must be literal contiguous spans from the original report. Quote enough
  context to establish anatomy and assertion, including relevant hedging.
